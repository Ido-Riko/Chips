# Smart Band live heart-rate capture

Android integration, 13 November 2026. Today → Heart rate and the assistant's Heart rate shortcut open the same capture screen. No CHIPS server, enrollment, internet or Health Connect is required for BLE capture or saved sessions. This absorbs the standalone converter's input/output functions into CHIPS; it does not uninstall that app, import its preferences, implement sleep/history import or correlate Beat Saber scores yet.

## Capture

Enable **Settings → Share HR** on the Xiaomi Smart Band 10. Allow nearby-device and notification access in CHIPS, choose **Find band**, select the intended device and choose **Start capture**. Android 8–11 scanning uses foreground location permission; Android 12+ uses Bluetooth scan/connect permissions without deriving location. The initial device picker scans only while visible, for up to 20 seconds. Find band shows its searching status immediately; Bluetooth adapter access, scan start/stop and device-name lookups run on a dedicated serial worker. Cancelling or leaving the screen invalidates callbacks immediately and queues radio cleanup, including when Android is slow to return from scan startup. Old scan results/errors cannot alter a later scan. Once capture starts, the ported converter owns reconnection and its 30-second fallback scans in the foreground service. It tries the chosen/last device first and retains the converter's Xiaomi/Mi Band name matching when a direct connection fails. Connection events retain the actual source address when it changes during recovery.

Capture is a non-exported `connectedDevice` foreground service. Its immediate, ongoing **CHIPS · HR capture is on** notification shows fresh BPM or connection/stale-data status and includes **Reconnect** and **Stop**. Tapping it reopens Heart rate. Notification access and an enabled channel are required before capture starts; disabling Bluetooth permissions or notifications during capture stops it on the next service check. Notification contents are private on the lockscreen. Android retains control over notification presentation/dismissal and its system Active apps Stop control.

Closing/recreating the screen or leaving the app does not stop the service. Stop uses the converter's graceful CCCD unsubscribe/disconnect and peripheral cleanup, with bounded final-close fallbacks. The notification remains visible as stopping while accepted samples drain and the radio cleanup finishes; a new session waits for both. The bounded/renewed partial wake lock follows the converter's connecting/connected/disconnecting policy and is released while idle, scanning or after Stop. An active capture uses Android's sticky foreground-service restart after ordinary process death. Its local restart settings restore the selected device and last applied outputs, reopen the same session UUID and record an interruption event; missing time remains a gap. The notification is rebuilt while the band reconnects. Restart timing remains under Android/Poco control. Explicit CHIPS Stop clears restart intent before shutdown; Android app Stop/force-stop and reboot require a new explicit Start. Unfinished sessions are marked **interrupted** at their last saved reception until a system restart resumes them. Restart settings are excluded from cloud backup/device transfer and never start capture merely because the app opens.

The reader uses standard Heart Rate service `0x180D`, measurement `0x2A37` and CCCD `0x2902`. It waits for subscription acknowledgement before receiving samples, handles 8-bit/16-bit values, ignores empty/truncated/zero/no-contact measurements, and supports legacy and API 33+ callbacks without double delivery. It retains the converter's 3-second initial wake-up attempt, 15-second connection timeout, 300 ms first retry, later capped delays, five-attempt direct retry budget, scan fallback after repeated direct failures and 4-second manual-scan fallback. Discovery/subscription now also has a 15-second timeout so a stalled subscription cannot leave the connection permanently stuck. The retry counter resets after a successful subscription or the converter's new/manual scan path. Exhausted retries leave the ongoing notification visible with a scan/reconnect instruction. Bluetooth OFF/ON handling restores the reader and any enabled peripheral. **Reconnect** gracefully restarts the converter's last-device/scan path; **Find band** can select a changed address after stopping. Old-connection callbacks and callbacks after Stop cannot add measurements.

## Local records

Independent `heart_rate.db` stores session UUID/start/end/status/source device, each valid sample's BPM, reception wall-clock milliseconds and Android monotonic elapsed milliseconds, and connection events. Schema v2 groups consecutive equal BPM in bounded runs of up to 256 samples, preserving the first timing plus lossless variable-length timing deltas and count. It splits runs at connection events, backwards elapsed time and reception gaps over 15 seconds. It does not assert continuous HR between readings. Existing v1 samples migrate transactionally without losing timestamps or session/event history. There is no sensor-origin timestamp claim. **Every accepted valid measurement is queued**, including repeated unchanged BPM; UI/notification cadence does not filter storage. The storage actor commits batches every two seconds or 32 measurements and flushes at connection/output changes and normal Stop. Abrupt process loss can discard the last roughly two seconds of uncommitted measurements; completed commits remain durable. Outputs and live UI receive measurements immediately. Compression reduces rows/timing bytes; batching reduces transaction frequency. Received-sample averages weight each run by its count, preserving the original sample statistics. Missing samples and disconnect events never become zero BPM. A bounded queue stops capture visibly rather than silently dropping measurements if storage cannot keep up.

Saved captures show received sample count, sample average and peak. These summarize received samples, not time-weighted full-session HR or inferred coverage. No health zones, sleep scores or causal coaching are generated. The most recent 100 sessions are listed; older rows remain stored. Confirmed Delete capture immediately removes its readings/events and identifying capture metadata locally, retaining only a deletion marker for synchronization. It does not remove activity history. Capturing does not automatically log a completed Workout or Beat Saber activity. The database follows Android app backup policy. Server synchronization is optional and uses the existing enrollment; an export UI and automatic Android archive restoration are not included.

## Stop, save and link Beat Saber

Explicit Stop first drains and saves HR locally, disables capture/recovery, finishes BLE cleanup and removes the ongoing notification. It then asks **Save a Beat Saber session too?** with **Save Beat Saber session** and **HR only**. Notification Stop opens the Heart rate screen through a non-exported activity so the same prompt can appear after shutdown; stopping from the screen uses the same persisted choice. Dismissing the prompt keeps HR only. Closing/restarting the app before choosing preserves the pending prompt. Disabling permissions or an ordinary process interruption does not invent a completed Beat Saber activity.

Confirming writes one local Beat Saber activity with a stable ID and an explicit `hr_session_id` link in the same activity row. The capture start instant and its original timezone determine the activity date, even across midnight or later timezone changes. The capture window is the association, not a claim that every moment was Beat Saber or that particular BeatLeader scores were played. No automatic score import/matching or overlap deduplication with a separately logged manual activity is inferred. Both the HR card and activity History can open the other save. Repeated confirmation, recreation and recovery after a crash between the activity commit and prompt acknowledgement cannot duplicate the completion.

**HR only** preserves the capture without creating an activity. Saved HR cards can offer the choice later. Deleting an activity retains its HR capture; deleting HR retains the activity and labels its capture unavailable. A deleted linked activity is never resurrected by retrying the save choice. Editing a linked activity keeps its Beat Saber type and stable link.

HR schema v3 adds the persisted prompt, capture timezone, finalization state, sync outbox and deletion markers. Activity schema v3 adds the unique HR link without altering practice or voice history. Existing HR v1/v2 and activity v1/v2 databases migrate additively. Legacy HR sessions use UTC because their original timezone was not stored. An interrupted capture awaiting possible sticky recovery is not uploaded as an immutable archive. If it never recovers, **Keep interrupted HR capture** finalizes the retained data for sync; saving it as Beat Saber also finalizes it. If recovery wins that race, stop the live capture first.

## Local-first server synchronization

1. HR samples, the stopped capture and the optional linked activity commit on the phone. Their UI never waits for server availability. Live capture does not need enrollment or internet.
2. With synchronization connected in Settings, WorkManager uploads finalized captures as an immutable manifest and bounded pages of 128 compressed runs/connection events. Each acknowledgement is checked against the sent content. The server marks an archive complete only after verifying all run, event and sample counts; partial uploads cannot be used by a newly linked activity.
3. The activity worker ensures its HR archive is complete before uploading the activity containing the capture UUID. Stable IDs and immutable chunks make retries after lost replies safe; local records remain pending/failed until acknowledged. Unlinked stopped HR captures also sync independently.
4. Offline/retryable failures retain local data and retry with network/backoff constraints. Authentication, identity/content conflicts and incompatible records surface errors for correction. Settings Retry sync retries both queues; Disconnect cancels/pauses HR and activity work. A restored app cannot send captures under a different endpoint/owner/device binding.

Delete queues an owner-scoped terminal server tombstone and removes server HR chunks/manifest when connected. A late upload cannot resurrect deleted HR. Concurrent local deletion cannot be cleared by an older upload acknowledgement. Activity tombstones retain the UUID relationship; deleting either side does not delete the other. HR and activity uploads are separate server transactions, so the archive may arrive before the linked activity; retries finish the second half without duplicates.

Deploy the updated backend and additive **005_heart_rate.sql** before accepting server storage. Authenticated routes under `/api/v1/heart-rate` accept the manifest, run/event pages, completion and deletion, and allow owner reads of completed archives/pages. Only the originating device may write/delete a capture or create its activity link. PostgreSQL stores compressed reception timing and connection events, including any source-address text in those events; the selected band's address is not a separate uploaded field. Include these archives and activity links in server backups. The client currently uploads its local archives; it does not automatically restore raw HR archives downloaded from another phone. A linked activity whose HR is absent locally remains visible with an unavailable-capture message.

## Optional converter outputs

Both outputs default off. During capture, edit the toggles or OSC host/port and choose **Apply outputs**. The applied configuration is shown separately from drafts. Changes preserve the session UUID and band reader. A replacement OSC target is resolved before closing the old sender; invalid targets leave the previous sender active and show an error. BLE off/on uses the original peripheral's graceful cleanup with a short bounded delay before restarting advertising. Advertising permission may be granted during capture; then tap Apply outputs again. A BLE peripheral restart can briefly disconnect relay clients while input capture continues.

- **Rebroadcast HR over Bluetooth:** opens a standard HR GATT service, advertises its UUID and forwards valid fresh samples to subscribed centrals, including 16-bit values. Requires advertising permission and peripheral support. The selected band's address is rejected as a relay client to avoid loopback. The original `requireBonding = true` behavior is restored: confirm Android's pairing prompt on the phone/receiver. The original bonding timeouts, saved bonded-client subscriptions (including the Quest fallback), loopback name/address checks, Device Information service, manufacturer scan response, advertising retry/restart and 20-second-on/10-second-off pulse mode remain. A missing/stale input uses the converter's no-HR output signal rather than disconnecting all bonded receivers or retaining an old positive value.
- **OSC:** configurable host/IP and port (default broadcast `255.255.255.255:9000`), address `/heartBeat`, integer type `,i`, big-endian value and NUL-terminated four-byte-aligned strings. Received samples are sent through the converter's own asynchronous sender with socket recovery and bounded shutdown. Its negative disconnect sentinel still becomes an output-only zero for receiver compatibility; it never enters the sample database. UDP delivery is unacknowledged. DNS/socket/output errors are visible and do not prevent local saving. Network operations run off the main thread.

## Provenance and acceptance

The current implementation directly ports `BleConnectionManager.kt`, `ConnectionStateMachine.kt`, `PeripheralManager.kt`, `AdvertisingConfig.kt` and `OscSender.kt` from [Ido-Riko/MiBand-Converter at a472d64](https://github.com/Ido-Riko/MiBand-Converter/tree/a472d64a6743454db0b63c902684be36426f2ff9), with the owner's explicit reuse authorization. The original classes live under `com.chips.heartrate.converter`; the small CHIPS adapters call them. The earlier replacement reader/peripheral implementation is superseded. The original Android service orchestration informed the Bluetooth receiver, wake-lock and shutdown integration; CHIPS retains its own persistent notification, Compose UI and local session store.

The [source-port diff](evidence/miband-converter-port.patch) records every change from that pinned source, including package/provenance headers. Integration changes are limited to:

- A separate raw-sample callback before the converter's unchanged-value suppression/500 ms presentation throttle. The original filtered callback remains; negative values never become stored samples. Legacy Android callbacks and correct wide-HR relay encoding are supported.
- Serial GATT callbacks on the original handler, stale-connection/delayed-attempt guards and explicit reconnect entry. Unexpected connected-device loss re-enters the original retry path; subscription failure/timeout also retries. The original connection timing and scan machinery remain.
- Bonding/pairing broadcasts use the platform's exported-system-receiver flag so Bluetooth's separate privileged process can deliver them. The selected band's address is excluded from peripheral orphan-cleanup disconnections, preserving the input link/Gadgetbridge path.
- Optional OSC errors reach CHIPS status. The sender retains its executor, socket recreation, protocol and shutdown.

Android platform contracts checked against [Bluetooth permissions](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/bluetooth/bt-permissions) and [foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types), [sticky service restart](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/Service#START_STICKY), [user-initiated service stopping](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/handle-user-stopping), and [Bluetooth/system broadcast receiver flags](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/broadcasts#context-registered-receivers).

Automated validation is recorded in the progress ledger. Physical acceptance still requires the actual Smart Band/Poco: enable Share HR, verify reception/sample cadence and stable HR values, screen-off capture, notification visibility/Stop, disconnect/reconnect and Bluetooth toggle, Gadgetbridge coexistence, actual Quest BLE subscription and the owner's OSC receiver. Do not retire the standalone converter until those outputs are accepted. The emulator tests exercise the original state machine, direct converter raw callbacks and reconnect cancellation, OSC socket recovery, and CHIPS service lifecycle/notification with injected transport. These do not establish sustained real band/Quest behavior.
